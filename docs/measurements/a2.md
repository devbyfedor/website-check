### One site: one more finding on Cloudflare

On Cloudflare the analytics tag of one site sent two more requests right after its page view, both answered: `stats.g.doubleclick.net/g/collect` (HTTP 204) and `www.google.it/ads/ga-audiences` (HTTP 200). They make the service "Google Analytics advertising features" (Google signals / remarketing audiences, `consent-required`). Locally there is no such request. The same site in every capture:
- Local captures from Germany (20.11.2026 and three times on 21.11.2026, plus the local capture of part 3): 5 of 5 have only the page view to `region1.google-analytics.com/g/collect`.
- Cloudflare captures from Milan: 2 of 3 have the two extra requests (part 2 strategy b, part 3), 1 of 3 does not (part 2 strategy a). There the page view went to `region1.analytics.google.com`.

So the extra finding is real (the requests went to Google's advertising service before consent), but it depends on where the browser runs: a visitor from Germany did not trigger it. Google Analytics itself is a finding on this site locally and on Cloudflare alike. This is the location risk noted since part 1: the browser runs in the EU, but not in Germany, and some services answer by country.

## Comparison of the plans

- **Plan A, browser sessions in the Worker within 10 ms CPU:** does not fit. A page costs 34 to 183 ms in strategy (a) and 13 to 21 ms in strategy (b) of Worker CPU (part 1). The free plan allows only occasional overruns of the 10 ms; constant overruns end with error 1102 (Workers limits page). Not one page stayed under 10 ms, not even a 2-request check page.
- **Plan B, Quick Actions:** cannot capture the page's requests. None of the endpoints returns the requests a page made (only the main document's status and headers), so third-party services cannot be seen. Not measured, as Fedor decided.
- **Plan C, more work in the page (strategy b):** cheaper (10 to 21 ms) but still over the Worker's 10 ms, and it loses findings: the page cannot see the HTTP status of cross-origin requests, and requests inside iframes or still open at the end are missing. On 2 of 4 sites findings became hints. Not usable without a new sourced rule.
- **Capture in a Durable Object (recommended):** the Worker only checks the request and passes it on (0 to 1 ms). The Durable Object has 30 s CPU per request on the free plan (Durable Objects limits page: "CPU per request: 30 seconds (default)", no split by plan; the free plan offers SQLite-backed objects only). Measured: 13 to 334 ms per page in part 3, 5 s of CPU without a stop in part 2, 48 of 48 events `ok` in part 3. Strategy (a) keeps all findings that the local capture has.

## Capacity of the free plan

A full check is the homepage and the Impressum, opened one after the other in the same browser. The Impressum was not measured in A2; the estimate gives it the same time as the homepage of the same site, which overestimates its time because Impressum pages are usually simpler. Browser time is counted from the start of the session to its close; outside the pages a session needs about 0.5 s to open and 0.1 s to close.

| Free limit | Use per full check (measured or estimated) | Full checks per day |
|---|---|---|
| Browser Run: 10 browser minutes per day | 2 pages x 4.7 s (mean) + 0.6 s = 10 s; median 7.6 s; worst case 2 x 15.3 s + 0.6 s = 31 s | **about 60** (79 at the median, 19 in the worst case) |
| Browser Run: 3 browsers at the same time, 1 new browser every 20 s | one browser per check, about 10 s | not a daily limit; at most 3 checks start per minute (a queue in A5) |
| Workers: 100,000 requests per day, 10 ms CPU per request | about 20 requests (start, polling the status, report) at 0 to 1 ms each | about 5,000 |
| Durable Objects: 100,000 requests per day | about 5 (open, two pages, close, an alarm) | about 20,000 |
| Durable Objects: 13,000 GB-s per day | 128 MB x (10 to 31 s of work + 30 s until the idle alarm) = 5 to 8 GB-s | about 1,600 to 2,500 |
| Durable Objects: 100,000 SQLite rows written per day | each `setAlarm` is one row: about 2 per check | about 50,000 |
| D1 (A5): 100,000 rows written per day | if every request were one row: about 150 per check at the A1 median of 74 requests per page, about 1,000 on the heaviest sites | about 100 to 650 |

The first limit to run out is Browser Run's 10 minutes per day: about 60 full checks per day. Our own tests count against the same 10 minutes (part 3 used 75.8 s). After the daily limit Browser Run answers with error 429 until 00:00 UTC; the brief plans the quick check without a browser for that case (A5). D1 is the second limit only if A5 stores every request as its own row; storing the requests of a scan as one JSON value keeps it far away.

## What costs money (note for Fedor only)

From Cloudflare's pricing pages (read on 21.11.2026). Nothing of this is switched on.
- Workers Paid: at least 5 US dollars per month. It includes 10 browser hours per month (then 0.09 dollars per hour) and 10 concurrent browsers (then 2 dollars per browser), no daily browser limit, 30 s CPU per Worker request by default (then plan A would fit too), 10 million Worker requests and 1 million Durable Object requests per month.
- 10 browser hours per month are about 20 minutes per day, so about twice the free daily minutes, spread over the month.
